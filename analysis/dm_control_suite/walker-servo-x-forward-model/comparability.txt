# REGRIDDING COST
##############################################################################
regridding cost on steps_to_900 (common grid minus native cadence)
  runs with both readouts : 72  (18 fine cadence, 54 coarse)
  all                     : mean +8.11e6  median +9.58e6  range [+0.00e6, +16.22e6]
  fine cadence (6e5)      : mean +11.03e6  median +10.81e6  range [+6.64e6, +16.22e6]
  coarse cadence (4.8e6)  : mean +7.13e6  median +9.58e6  range [+0.00e6, +14.50e6]

  The number that matters is the *difference* between those two groups,
  because cadence is confounded with the manipulation: every servo run is
  coarse and most torque runs are fine. It is +3.90e6 steps --
  i.e. under the native definition the torque arms' crossings would sit
  that much earlier *relative to* the servo arms than they do here.
  Against a 1e9-step budget and arm differences of 1e8-5e8 steps that is
  a percent-scale correction, but it points the wrong way if left out.

  The two definitions also differ in window (2.4e7 vs 1e7), and a
  wider trailing window lags a rising curve, so the shift above is
  the *combined* cost of matching both siblings' conventions rather
  than grid granularity alone.
